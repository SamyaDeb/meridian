# Meridian: Monad's funding-rate layer

*Monad Metropolis · Track 01 Onchain Finance & Trading · draft 7 Oct 2026 (submission by Mon 12 Oct). Every number here is traceable in [`PROOF.md`](PROOF.md) or [`BENCHMARKS.md`](BENCHMARKS.md). Status: **live on Monad testnet; mainnet after the hackathon.***

## The problem
Perpetual funding is paid every 1–8 hours because the chains and venues that set the convention were slow. Hourly funding is a countdown, not a price: a position opened a minute before the tick pays a full period, and the rate everyone quotes is a stale average. There is also no onchain funding *rate* that other protocols can read: anyone who wants to earn the basis or fix their funding cost has to import a CEX number through an oracle and settle per period.

On Monad, blocks are ≈ 300 ms (measured 300–304 ms on testnet in our bench runs). Funding can be continuous, and the rate can be a public onchain primitive.

## The product: trade it, earn it, fix it, one rate, one engine
- **Trade it.** MON-PERP on a **fully onchain order book** (no offchain matching engine), isolated margin up to 5x, collateral AUSD. **Funding accrues every block** through O(1) cumulative indices; mark = median of index-implied, book mid and last trade, with the book-driven premium rate-limited per block. Liquidations, an insurance fund, and a bad-debt waterfall in which protocol fees are junior to users.
- **A venue, not one market.** Every market is its own order book + clearinghouse + funding index, registered in one factory and backed by one insurance fund. On testnet **MON-PERP and ETH-PERP** run side by side (ETH-PERP added on 8 Oct without touching a deployed contract, PHASES 44); a trade, per-block funding and a liquidation on ETH-PERP are onchain, and the B4 benchmark shows the two markets' order flow in the same blocks touches **no common storage slot**, which is what lets Monad execute them in parallel (PROOF §K7, BENCHMARKS B4; run on an anvil fork of testnet, not on testnet).
- **The oracle.** The clearinghouse is a **public funding-rate oracle**: `averageFundingRate(blocksAgo)` / `averageFundingApr` = ΔF / ΔG over any window, `observe`, `latestObservation`. Any Monad contract can read the realized funding rate, without importing it. The app's `/rates` page (Meridian Funding Index) shows realized 1 h / 8 h / 24 h APR.
- **Earn it.** A basis vault (ERC-4626 + an ERC-7540-style queue): spot MON plus a short perp, sized by the net long skew, so depositors earn the floating funding. Small exits are instant from idle; larger ones are queued, priced at NAV, and fulfilled by the keeper with a routed spot sale, and the batch pays its own unwind, so remaining holders aren't diluted. In the app, a withdrawal beyond idle becomes a request with its place in the queue and a labelled estimate, then Cancel or Claim (`app/e2e/earn-queue-anvil.spec.ts`). The owner (a Safe on mainnet) can `emergencyUnwind`.
- **Fix it.** 30-day fixed-for-floating funding swaps on Meridian's own order book (price = 1 + APR). The floating leg settles from the same per-block funding index; traders lock their funding cost, yield seekers lock a fixed rate. **On testnet a second swap market settles on Perpl's MON funding** (another perp DEX on Monad): a small adapter snapshots Perpl's onchain funding sum once per Perpl interval and serves it through the same oracle views, so a Perpl long can lock its funding cost on Meridian with the swap contract unchanged. It trusts Perpl's funding data (admin-overwritable, upgradeable proxy) and accrues only when poked (`docs/PERPL_SWAPS.md`, PROOF §15).

## Why only Monad
1. **Risk scales with √τ.** Margin has to cover the price move over the time τ before risk can be re-assessed: z·σ·√τ. At σ = 100%: one Monad block (τ = 0.3 s) needs 0.029%, an 8-hour funding interval 9.066% (BENCHMARKS B6, `bench/sqrt_tau.py`, MATH_SPEC §3.4). Here funding, mark and liquidation eligibility all update every block. (Honesty caveat from MATH_SPEC §3.4: continuous-liquidation venues elsewhere have τ = keeper latency, not the funding interval.)
2. **Per-block funding is cheap enough to run every block.** The clearinghouse's first funding touch in a block costs **29,452 gas** (asserted < 30,000; 29,867 with the mark bound active; 30,168 on the 1-in-200 checkpoint block). Place + cancel on the onchain book costs **96,484** (< 100,000). Foundry, Cancun schedule; Monad testnet receipts are higher because MIP-8 prices cold storage pages and Monad bills the gas *limit*: BENCHMARKS B1 shows execution estimate vs billed limit per op.
3. **Measured latency.** On testnet a −15% oracle update was followed by the leveraged long's liquidation **4–5 blocks** after it became eligible (≈ 1.5 s; three runs on the current deployment with the keeper on a free-tier RPC; 8 blocks on the earlier deployment over the public RPC; 1 block on a local node, BENCHMARKS B7). The keeper then closed the position it inherited **7 blocks** later. Our ≤ 3-block target needs a paid RPC for the keeper; we report the miss rather than hide it.

## Architecture
```
            Pyth MON/USD (mainnet) ──► OracleAdapter (testnet: keeper-fed TestnetKeeperOracle)
                                            │ index
 traders / MM bot ──► OrderBook (CLOB) ──► ClearingHouse ── funding every block (O(1) F, G indices)
                       fills, Skip/Evict      │   │  mark = median(index·(1+premium), mid, last), premium ≤ 10 bps/block
                                              │   ├─► IFundingRateOracle: averageFundingRate / observe  ──► anyone
                                              │   ├─► InsuranceFund ◄── liquidation penalties; bad debt: IF → fees → uncovered
 keeper (TS) ── oracle push, funding poke, ───┘   │
   liquidations sized to headroom + reduce-only    ├─► FundingSwapMarket + swap OrderBook (fixed vs floating, settles on F)
   unwind, vault fulfil/rebalance                  └─► MeridianVault v2 (idle AUSD + KuruHedger spot + short perp; request/fulfil/claim queue)
                                                              └─► Kuru Flow routes for the spot leg (mainnet)
 Envio HyperIndex ◄── events ──► Next.js PWA (Privy login when configured): Trade · Earn · Fix · Rates · Stats
```
Contracts are non-upgradeable (Solidity 0.8.28, Cancun). Every loop is bounded by a constant; no global iteration over users, positions or orders.

## Bounties

- **Envio, Best Use of Envio:** HyperIndex is Meridian's data layer, and it does the math traders read instead of the browser. **Derived in handlers:** realized funding APR over 1 h / 8 h / 24 h / 7 d (a bounded mirror of the oracle's checkpoint ring, searched like `observe()`, so it equals `averageFundingApr` even on a quiet market); funding paid, realized PnL, fees and liquidations per account, with one row per funding payment (conservation checked on recorded chain data); the insurance-fund waterfall, one row per step (IF → fees → uncovered); daily protocol stats with active external users (our own bots excluded); the vault v2 redemption queue with timestamps. **Cross-venue:** the same indexer follows **Perpl's exchange** on Monad testnet and, as a second chain, on **Monad mainnet** (multichain config). It produces an hourly Meridian-vs-Perpl funding series (`CrossVenueFunding`) that `/rates` charts from one GraphQL endpoint. We indexed only events we had seen decode on the deployed contract, proved Perpl's funding unit against its own API (63/63 events), and replayed indexed positions against the exchange's `getPosition` (0 mismatches). Numbers from the hosted deployment (sync time, lag at head, query latency): > VERIFY after deployment (PROOF §11). Evidence: `indexer/PERPL_EVENTS.md`, `indexer/SCHEMA.md`, PROOF §11. - **Privy:** Privy is the app's single auth layer and it fixes a perp's worst step, onboarding. What we use: **login** with email, passkey, Google or an external wallet (one Privy modal); **embedded wallets** created for every user without one; **native gas sponsorship** on Monad Testnet (`sendTransaction(…, { sponsor: true })`, EIP-7702, embedded wallets only, kill switch `NEXT_PUBLIC_SPONSOR_GAS=0`, never on mainnet); **export wallet** from the account menu. Why it matters for a perp: before, a tester needed a wallet, MON for gas and AUSD before their first order; now it is **email → embedded wallet → free test AUSD + deposit (one tap, three sponsored transactions) → first filled market order**, measured on Monad Testnet with a fresh Privy user: **5 taps, 22.6 s, 0 MON before and after** (Start trading, email Submit, ToS checkbox, one setup tap, Market long; PROOF §10 has the four tx hashes), and **4 taps / 19.6 s** on anvil without the Privy modal (`app/e2e/onboarding-anvil.spec.ts`). Design: Privy loads only in builds with an app ID; the write path is one core (estimate × 1.2 gas limit, receipt check incl. the bundled call's own result) with two transports (`app/src/lib/tx.ts`, `sponsor.ts`). Demo video: [timestamp TBD].
- **Chainlink, Best workflow with CRE:** a perp is only as safe as its keeper, and ours was one TS process with one key. Three CRE workflows make it a backstop that doesn't depend on that process: **W1 liquidation guardian** (oracle `PriceUpdated` log trigger + cron fallback → confirm liquidatable accounts with EVM reads → `LIQUIDATE`, then `UNWIND` so the receiver doesn't keep the risk), **W2 upkeep** (funding poke when the TS keeper falls ≥ 40 blocks behind; vault rebalance, dry-run first), **W3 MFI publisher** (Meridian's onchain funding rate + Perpl's rate over HTTP under DON consensus → the Meridian Funding Index onchain in `MeridianMfiFeed`, readable by any contract). All three report to one receiver contract (`MeridianKeeperReceiver`) that isolates every item (a failing target never reverts the report) and supports workflow-identity checks for production. Evidence on Monad testnet, 8 Oct (PROOF §13): funding pokes, 4 MFI publishes that match our MFI API to the documented weight difference, and 4 liquidations of a labelled test wallet on the demo market, each followed by a full unwind, receiver flat, with no TS keeper on that market. Liquidated 9–13 blocks after eligibility, vs 4–5 for the TS keeper (BENCHMARKS B7). **Exactly what ran:** `cre workflow simulate --broadcast`, the real workflow WASM on one machine through Chainlink's simulation forwarder, **not a DON** (deployment access not granted at submission; moving is `setForwarder` + `setExpectedWorkflow`, keepers/cre/README.md). Demo video: [timestamp TBD].
- **Perpl, Best Analytics / Risk Tool:** **Rates & Risk** (`/rates`) is the page a Perpl trader opens next to Perpl. *Why:* Perpl shows your own position; it can't tell you what funding costs on the other Monad venue, or how close a wallet you follow is to liquidation. *What:* (1) Meridian (funding every block) vs Perpl (every 8,571 blocks) on Perpl testnet or mainnet: rate now, realized 1 h / 8 h / 24 h / 7 d, the spread and its history, the MFI line, a countdown to Perpl's next funding event in blocks, and the net carry of a delta-neutral pair after both venues' taker fees; (2) a **position monitor for any Perpl wallet**: positions read onchain from Perpl's exchange (`getPositionV2` and friends, one multicall), the liquidation price computed with **Perpl's own rule** (from Perpl's SDK, whose test vectors our module passes), distance to liquidation, ±5 / 10 / 15 % shocks, funding per day at Perpl's rate and at the MFI, and a one-tap "Offset on Meridian" that prefills our ticket (never submits); shareable links `?perpl=<wallet>&chain=…`; (3) Meridian's own risk: users' liquidation-distance histogram, OI and skew, our MM's share disclosed, the insurance-fund waterfall and a block-by-block crash replay labelled "local test market"; (4) an open-source alerts bot that pages Discord / Telegram when a watched Perpl position nears liquidation or the spread passes a threshold. *Evidence:* PROOF §14 (formula sources, recorded reads, rounding, alerts, Lighthouse mobile 97 / 100); our-vs-Perpl comparison for one testnet and one mainnet account: > VERIFY (owner screenshots / perpl-cli, PROOF §14). Demo video: [timestamp TBD].
- **Perpl, Best API usage:** Meridian's market maker hedges its leftover inventory on Perpl through Perpl's authenticated trading API, so the perp it makes markets on keeps two-sided quotes; the Meridian Funding Index reads Perpl live. What we use: **API-key auth** (Ed25519, signed REST headers and the signed `mt: 29` sign-in on the trading WebSocket; test vectors in `mm-bot/test/perpl-auth.test.ts`); **order forwarding** (`fw` checked on every account update; startup refuses without it); **orders over the trading WebSocket** (`mt: 22`, request ids seeded from `lfr`, `lb` from the heartbeat head + the market's `order_ttl_blocks`, IOC hedges) with a signed **`POST /v1/trading/orders`** fallback; **confirmation from the streams**, never from the ack (`mt: 3` admission, `mt: 24` orders with Perpl's dedup rules, `mt: 25` fills, `mt: 26/27` positions, `mt: 19/21` account, heartbeat sequence with reconnect on a gap), reconciled from `GET /v1/trading/{wallet,orders,positions}` after any reconnect; a **rate limiter** under the 60/min testnet limit and one trading connection (4 per wallet); the **market-data WebSocket** (`funding@`, `market-state@`, `market-config@`, `heartbeat@`) for the MFI and the hedge's prices, with the REST funding history as backfill/fallback; fees from `MarketConfig`. Why it matters for the perp: an unhedged MM widens and goes one-sided as inventory builds; hedged, it quotes on the net delta. Measured on Monad testnet, two 30-minute halves with the same house flow (7 Oct; PROOF §12): time one-sided **46.6% → 8.4%**, depth within ±1% **5.3/2.7 → 6.9/4.8 lots** (bid/ask), |net delta| p90 **2,500 → 500 MON**, with **2 hedges, both filled** (Perpl orders `4525679575056` and `4525855211536`, 2,000 and 600 MON, read back from `GET /v1/trading/fills`). The inner spread was wider in the hedged half (p50 5.41 → 6.96 bps): realized volatility was higher then, and an A–S spread has no inventory term, so we don't claim a tighter spread. A small sample, not the ≥ 1 h we planned. Kill switch, circuit breaker and testnet-only guardrails in `docs/PERPL_HEDGE.md`. The MFI also publishes the **Meridian–Perpl funding spread** (`/v1/mfi/spread`). Demo video: [timestamp TBD].
